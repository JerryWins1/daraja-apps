Your day is a playlist. The NOW task is the track that's playing — big art, progress bar, and three controls everyone on earth already knows: ✓ done (next track auto-plays), ⏭ not now (back of the queue), 🔀 pick for me. Routines are playlists: ☀️ Morning, 📚 Homework, 🌙 Bedtime. Parked thoughts? The B-sides. ✓ Zero learning curve — it's a music player. "One thing at a time" isn't a rule here, it's the physics of the interface. ✗ The queue hides the full day; planning lives in the playlists drawer.

My honest vote this round: C · Now Playing for grown-ups, B · The Trail as kid mode — same engine, two faces. Now Playing is the boldest idea that needs no explanation: everyone already knows what ▶ ✓ ⏭ do, so the ADHD philosophy (one track at a time, skipping is allowed, the queue waits quietly) comes free with the metaphor. The Trail turns the same day into a place kids want to walk. And the River (A) remains the safest single answer if you want one interface for everyone.
